# Valuation 1.2: mandatory calculation rules

## Evidence and image intake
Retain every existing workflow and output in SKILL.md. Extract image values with field, raw value, normalized value, currency/unit, period, source, and confidence. Mark unreadable or absent values as missing, never zero. Separate historical facts, sourced forecasts and analyst assumptions. Seek company IR, audited consolidated statements and exchange filings; record URL, publication date, reporting period and page/table. Check balance-sheet identity, cash-flow reconciliation, CAPEX signs, TTM versus annual periods, parent versus consolidated amounts and share adjustments. Do not manufacture missing inputs or silently infer CFO from earnings multiples. If inputs remain unavailable, show N/A with the exact missing item; compute only supported methods. Conditional scenarios require explicit assumptions and must not masquerade as factual valuations.

## Asset approach
NWC = current assets - current liabilities; NWC per share measures liquidity, not fair equity value. NCAV = current assets - ALL liabilities. For common-share NCAV, also deduct preferred claims and noncontrolling interests if applicable and not already included; use consistent consolidation scope. Divide by current diluted shares, explaining the denominator. Retain negative values but do not give a positive buy threshold or MOS for them. The 2/3 NCAV screen applies only to positive NCAV and is not a guarantee of liquidation proceeds. For three scenarios, show sourced book NCAV plus transparent recovery haircuts on inventory/receivables and liquidation costs; never invent book balances. Do not apply earnings growth to NCAV. Flag banks/insurers and real estate project inventories as requiring sector-specific interpretation.

## Cash flow definitions and ten-year DCF
Use CF0 as normalized most recent annual cash flow. Forecast t=1..10 recursively: CF_t=CF_(t-1)*(1+g_t). For changing growth, use the product of all annual growth factors, never CF0*(1+g_t)^t. Forecast reinvestment, working capital and debt consistently with growth.
FCFF = EBIT*(1-tax rate)+D&A-total CAPEX-change in operating NWC. Discount with WACC.
FCFE = net income to common equity+D&A-other required reinvestment adjustments-total CAPEX-change in operating NWC+net borrowing, with clearly reconciled noncash adjustments. Alternatively use CFO-total CAPEX+net borrowing only after aligning interest/tax classifications and equity scope. CFO-CAPEX alone is a cash-flow proxy, not automatically FCFE; state debt and distribution constraints. Operating NWC excludes cash, financial investments and financing debt.
PV10=sum(CF_t/(1+r)^t, t=1..10). TV10=CF11/(r-g_terminal), where CF11 is normalized for sustainable reinvestment and r>g_terminal. Value=PV10+TV10/(1+r)^10. Show PV10, PV(TV), terminal share of value, equity bridge and per-share value separately. Ten years is the explicit forecast horizon, never a claim about payback. Show a ten-year-only value without TV as a separate subtotal, not full going-concern value.
For FCFF: common equity=enterprise value+excess cash+nonoperating assets-interest-bearing debt-other senior claims-NCI, avoiding overlap. For FCFE: do not subtract debt again; add only assets explicitly excluded from projected cash flows, once. Discount rates must match currency, nominal/real basis and cash-flow risk. Do not force a generic model on financial institutions.

## Owner Earnings
OE=normalized earnings attributable to common owners+appropriate attributable D&A/other noncash adjustments-maintenance CAPEX-incremental operating working capital needed to maintain competitive position and unit volume. Align all terms to the same ownership scope; never add full consolidated D&A to parent-only earnings without reconciling NCI. Do not blindly add back recurring stock compensation without accounting for dilution/economic cost.
Separate total CAPEX into maintenance and growth only when disclosures support it. If undisclosed, label maintenance estimates as scenario assumptions; do not equate maintenance to depreciation automatically. CFO already includes working-capital changes: when bridging from CFO, do not subtract those changes twice. Reconcile OE versus CFO-total CAPEX and explain differences.
OE before growth reinvestment represents maintainable earning power, not necessarily distributable cash while expanding. A growing OE valuation/payback must deduct incremental growth CAPEX and working capital needed for that growth, or provide an explicit funded-growth reconciliation; label raw OE payback as theoretical earning-power payback. Never combine high growth with zero growth funding by default. Use cost of equity for equity OE, account for debt service/financing, and apply the same ten-year DCF/terminal discipline when valuing OE.

## Payback: separate from valuation
Use P>0 and equity CF per share; never compare FCFF per share to a stock price. Keep base cash flows, share dilution, reinvestment and annual growth paths consistent between comparisons. Show FCF/FCFE and OE separately for all three scenarios, with 0% growth, expected growth and discounted expected-growth payback; also show user-requested growth as an explicitly justified forecast or sensitivity.
With positive constant CF0 and no growth, simple payback=P/CF0. With constant g, CF_t=CF0*(1+g)^t; cumulative undiscounted flow=CF0*(1+g)*((1+g)^N-1)/g for g!=0, otherwise N*CF0. For variable growth use the recursive year-by-year table.
Discounted cumulative flow=sum(CF_t/(1+r)^t). Find the first whole year crossing P. Optional fractional estimate=(N-1)+(P-cumulative_(N-1))/flow_N; use discounted flow_N for discounted payback and label interpolation approximate. The actual end-of-year receipt convention crosses at integer N.
Never include terminal value, resale proceeds or NCAV in operating-cash-flow payback. Default to a 10-year payback table; if not crossed, say 'chưa hoàn vốn trong 10 năm'. Extend beyond ten years only with disclosed post-year-10 assumptions and a stated cap; do not silently sustain short-term growth forever. Negative CF0 invalidates simple division, but a sourced turnaround forecast can still be evaluated cumulatively. If cash flows turn negative after first crossing, disclose reversal rather than implying permanent recovery.
For a strictly positive constant-growth perpetuity with -1<g<r and r>0, total discounted inflow=CF0*(1+g)/(r-g). If P is greater than OR equal to this limit, no finite discounted payback exists; equality approaches only asymptotically. For g=r, discounted annual CF equals CF0, so payback remains finite for finite P. Do not apply the perpetuity test to a finite forecast or a changing-growth path. Economic cash-generation payback is not dividends actually received and does not guarantee a market-price recovery.
